Combat hard water

Hertfordshire's water supply is incredibly hard due to the underlying chalk aquifer. Install a water softener to protect your entire plumbing system and prevent limescale from choking your boiler.

Lower the hot water temperature

Adjust your hot water cylinder or combi boiler temperature down to 60°C to significantly reduce the rate at which damaging limescale precipitates onto heating elements.

Monitor boiler pressure

Low pressure is a leading cause of heating failures across the county. Keep an eye on the gauge—it should ideally sit between 1 and 1.5 bar when cold.

Insulate external condensate pipes

Winter freeze-ups are highly common. Wrap the white plastic condensate pipe outside your house in foam insulation to prevent it from freezing and shutting down your boiler.

Install a magnetic system filter

Given the hard water in areas across East Herts, a magnetic filter is highly valuable for catching scale and metallic sludge before it can enter and damage the boiler.

Locate your internal stopcock

Know exactly where your main water shut-off valve is located (often under the kitchen sink) so you can isolate the supply immediately during a burst pipe or severe leak.

Bleed radiators regularly

If your radiators are cold at the top and warm at the bottom, trapped air is preventing proper circulation. Use a radiator key to release the air.

Descale showerheads and taps

Visible white chalky deposits on fixtures will quickly reduce water flow. Clean these regularly with a descaling solution to maintain pressure.

Isolate outside taps in winter

Prevent winter bursts by isolating the water supply to your garden tap, draining the excess water, and disconnecting any hoses before the first frost.

Listen for "kettling" noises

If your boiler sounds like a boiling kettle, it is likely suffering from a limescale build-up on the heat exchanger. Do not ignore this, as it forces the system to work harder.

Maintain a minimum overnight temperature

Keep your heating set to at least 14°C overnight during cold snaps to prevent internal pipes from freezing.

Consider a powerflush

For older heating systems, especially in period properties in St Albans, powerflushing forces a chemical cleaning additive through the pipes to remove built-up sludge and restore radiator efficiency.

Check the simple things first

Before calling an emergency callout for a dead boiler, verify that you haven't simply experienced a power cut, tripped a fuse, or run out of battery power in your wireless thermostat.

Do not pour fats down the sink

Blocked drains are a frequent callout. Never pour cooking oils or fats down the drain, as they solidify quickly and create severe blockages.

Address dripping taps promptly

A constant drip is usually caused by a degraded washer or O-ring. Replacing this cheap component saves significant water over the year.

Thaw frozen pipes safely

If a condensate pipe does freeze, never use a naked flame. Use a hot water bottle and work your way from the outlet backwards toward the house.

Fix running toilets

A toilet that constantly trickles water into the bowl often has a faulty flapper valve. This simple fix prevents massive hidden water waste and higher bills.

Schedule annual servicing

Keep your boiler warranty valid and ensure it is operating safely by booking an annual service with a local Gas Safe registered engineer.

Upgrade your thermostat controls

Upgrading to a smart thermostat allows you to precisely control when your heating is active, making it more cost-effective than leaving the heating on all day.

Never attempt gas repairs yourself

Whether you live in a modern build in Stevenage or an older home, it is illegal and dangerous to remove the casing of a gas boiler if you are not on the Gas Safe register.
